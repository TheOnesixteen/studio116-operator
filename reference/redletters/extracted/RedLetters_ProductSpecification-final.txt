1. Product Definition
RedLetters is a closed-corpus research publication that produces one human-reviewed finding per day from the spoken words attributed to Jesus in Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John. The corpus never expands. The product deepens over time by finding clearer, harder, and more unexpected connections inside the same finite body of text.
The intended emotional response is: I have read those words many times and never noticed that. The product delivers intellectual surprise, not devotional instruction.

2. Product Promise and Positioning
Primary line: A research publication. One finding per day from the words of Jesus. Human-reviewed.
Founder voice: A serious, strange, beautiful experiment in reading the red letters as a closed corpus.
Trust signal: Every finding is human-reviewed and labeled by confidence, safety level, source verses, evidence, and review status.
Growth hook: Subscribe to receive one complete finding each morning.
Long arc: After 365 findings, the archive becomes a manuscript-grade body of work.
3. Audience and Voice
RedLetters is written for three groups at once: believers who want to go deeper than ordinary devotional material, skeptics curious about the actual words versus later religious systems, and scholars or students who want a rigorous research companion.
The working voice is the skeptical believer: serious, curious, careful, and allergic to easy answers. A finding should hook like a headline and prove itself like a paper.

5. Methodology and Credibility
A finding is a specific, sourced, defensible observation about the text. It observes; it does not conclude. It may be numerical, linguistic, structural, historical, narrative, cross-gospel, or unexpectedly connected, but it must remain traceable to the corpus and honest about certainty.

Verification engine: Every quantitative or pattern-based finding must have a reproducible verification run stored with method, query, result, and timestamp. No verification run means the finding cannot become Reviewed.

Safety level
Distance from text

Each public finding displays confidence and safety level visibly.

Speculative findings must be labeled as speculative in both data and display copy.

8. Finding Categories
Numerical / Statistical: word counts, question ratios, frequency patterns.
Linguistic / Wordplay: unique words, untranslated terms, self-reference patterns.
Pattern / Structural: behavioral rhythms, rhetorical structures, repeated sequences.
Contrast / Tension: public versus private, once versus repeated, tone shifts.
Historical / Cultural: Roman law, Jewish practice, Greek or Aramaic context.
Narrative / Dramatic: interruptions, silences, crowd misunderstandings.
Philosophical / Unexpected: cross-disciplinary connections, clearly bounded.
Cross-Gospel: what appears in one gospel, all four gospels, or shifts between accounts.

14. AI Research Engine Prompt
Store the active prompt at prompts/v1_system_prompt.txt. Every generation call includes this prompt verbatim, followed by the runtime context block.
You are the RedLetters Research Engine.  Your role is to generate candidate research findings from a closed corpus: the spoken words attributed to Jesus in Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John (KJV). You are a research assistant. You do not publish. You generate candidates for human review. Nothing you produce becomes public without editorial approval.  Analyze only red-letter text from the four canonical gospels. No Revelation, Acts, epistles, apocrypha, narrator descriptions, or retrospective quotations. The corpus is fixed. That constraint is the product.  A finding is a specific, sourced, defensible observation about the text. It is not a devotional thought, sermon illustration, pastoral instruction, or theological conclusion. It observes; it does not conclude.  Assign category, confidence, and safety level. When uncertain, choose the more conservative level. Never overstate certainty. Never make unsourced quantitative claims. Never restate a finding already published or rejected. Never use phrases such as 'this shows us,' 'we should,' or 'God wants.'  Depth dial: - Early: favor Level 1-2 textual and cross-gospel findings. - Mid: favor Level 2-3 patterns and historical context. - Deep: favor Level 3-4 interpretive and behavioral observations. - Speculative: Level 4-5 allowed only when clearly labeled.  Output JSON only: {   "title": "short named finding, 3 to 7 words",   "hook": "one surprising, defensible, quotable sentence",   "insight": "80 to 150 words; observation first, evidence second",   "source_verses": ["Matthew 5:3", "Luke 6:20"],   "evidence_notes": "how the claim was checked or what rule applies",   "confidence": "Verified | Probable | Interpretive | Speculative",   "safety_level": 1,   "category_primary": "one allowed category",   "category_secondary": "second category or null",   "greek_note": "Greek insight or null",   "verification_needed": true,   "generation_notes": "caveats, counterexamples checked, confidence reasoning" }  Runtime context block appended after this prompt: - Corpus stats: word count, verse/segment count, included books. - Depth dial: current setting. - Published/scheduled findings: titles and source verses to avoid repetition. - Rejected topics: titles/themes and rejection reasons. - Pending verification: claims that need dataset work. 
15. Phase 2 Product Vision
Phase 2 is the full visual experience after the research engine is validated and the audience is real. Do not build it before the MVP proves daily findings, review, trust, subscription, and sharing.
Corpus strip: every red-letter word in canonical order, glowing as findings reference segments.
Constellation: each published finding is a star, clustered by category and difficulty.
Frontier indicator: percentage of corpus explored based on referenced segments.
Difficulty arc: visible movement from obvious to nuanced to obscure to unexpected.
Book export: after 365 reviewed findings, generate manuscript-ready output.
16. Legal Disclaimer
Required disclaimer: RedLetters findings are research observations based on textual analysis of the King James Version of the four canonical gospels. They are not pastoral advice, doctrinal authority, or the position of any church, denomination, or theological institution. Confidence and safety levels are disclosed on every finding. Speculative findings are labeled as such.
